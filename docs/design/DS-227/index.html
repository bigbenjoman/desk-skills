desk-q: keeping batches moving

desk-q shows whether passes are running, but not who runs them, whether anything actually moved, how much of your usage is left, or whether a waiting ticket will move without you. Below are the full screens as they would look, then every case against what desk-q does today. Nothing is built until you approve it.

✓ approved 24 Oct 2026

Design audit: the previous mock-ups

4/10The content was right; the screens had to be read, not scanned. Header lines were one run of white text, and red meant three different things.

Critical

  1. "Needs you" was red. desk-q draws it magenta (35). Red also marked failed and "supervisor off", so the one alarm colour no longer meant "nothing can run".
  2. The home header was a blob. Labels and figures had the same weight and colour, so 51, 18 and 12.1 GiB all looked alike. You had to read 100 characters to find one number.
  3. "oldest 16h" sat after "12 blocked", so it read as the oldest blocked ticket. It is the oldest ticket needing you (R8).

Important

  1. Blocked was amber, the same as waiting, which hid the one difference the W cases turn on. desk-q draws blocked dim.
  2. Table counts were all plain: 0 looked like 28. desk-q colours each count by its state and dims a zero.
  3. The repo header had no usage, gave only one state count, and buried who runs the batch mid-sentence.
  4. Notes mixed their signals (a dim ▲, repo names at varying positions), and the ticket screens' right-hand hints drifted by up to 4 columns.

What works

  1. Every state starts with a glyph, so --plain still reads.
  2. Warnings go in two places only: the red band and the notes.
  3. LAST PASS answers "is it moving, and who moves it" in one cell.
  4. The ticket screen's fixed order, and real figures throughout.

Rules the redesign follows

  • Your status line's language. Labels dim, values bright, states in their colour, segments split by a dim │.
  • Order by what you scan for: what needs you, then whether work is moving, then capacity and usage.
  • One colour, one meaning, and a glyph in front of every coloured run. Bold is for what you act on: commands, the selected row, needs-you counts. A zero is grey.
  • Every piece of text passes WCAG AA (4.5:1), on the window background and on the selected row: labels 5.1:1 and 4.6:1, magenta 5.7:1 and 5.1:1, red 5.3:1, the band's text 6.4:1, everything else 5.9:1 to 11.5:1. Labels are a fixed grey, not the terminal's dim (SGR 2), whose shade each terminal picks for itself: desk-q draws them in 256-colour grey 247, which is 5.2:1 on this theme and 7.8:1 on black, and in a darker grey on a light terminal theme.
◆ needs youmagenta: nothing moves until you act
● runninggreen: moving; also a live pass and a merged blocker ✓
◐ landingcyan: a PR open and merging
○ readyblue: starts when a worker is free
· waiting ▲amber: moves by itself, or a warning worth knowing
◌ blockeddim: held outside the batch; dim is also every label
✕ failedred: failed, errors, a batch stopped over a day, and the band when nothing can run
██████████usage under 60%
██████████60–84%, or weekly pace holding workers
██████████85% and over

Four rules the screens follow

  • Who runs the batch replaces the word "live" in LAST PASS: ● supervisor or ● session. It reads the per-repo §3b setting "Batches run by" from DS-204, and warns only when the setting and reality disagree, or when the supervisor has stopped.
  • Live means something moved. A pass that ran but started, merged and released nothing for an hour reads ▲ no progress 1h, with the reason it held (weekly pace, memory, maintenance, a failed sign-in) in the notes.
  • Usage is one line in every header: 5-hour and 7-day bars with reset times. Each bar and its percentage turn from green to amber at 60% (or when weekly pace holds workers) and to red at 85%.
  • A state says whether it moves without you. · waiting moves by itself. ◆ needs you means nothing moves until you act, and the detail names the action. ◌ blocked is held by something outside the batch.

The colour key is in the audit above. Warnings live in two places only: one red band at the top when nothing can move, and a quiet notes area above the key bar for everything else.

4 · One ticket, opened

The ticket screen gets a fixed order. First, one coloured line saying whether the ticket will move without you. Then the blockers, grouped by who has to act and coloured the same way as the states. Then any warnings, then timing, then the detail, with "Why lane" folded to two lines.

Colours follow the key in the audit: green merged or moving, magenta needs you, amber a warning, dim outside the batch. The rail ▌ takes the ticket's state colour. Every coloured line also starts with a glyph, so it reads without colour.

How desk-q knows, with no model and no tokens

The setting

The repo's "Batches run by" row, read like the other config rows.

Handed over

The queue's supervised flag, already read for the resume hint.

Supervisor on and off

The new supervisor command and s key call supervisor.mjs install and uninstall, as the + - keys already write workers.

Agent loaded

One launchctl print per repo with a batch, per refresh.

Drafts and merges

Not recorded today: the pass fetches only a PR's state, URL and checks. It must also record draft, review decision and mergeable, which W3 depends on.

Progress and holds

New: each pass records its status, what it held and why, and when anything last started, merged or was released. desk-q reads it; it never works it out.

Usage

The newest of: a worker log's rate_limit_event, the status line's reading, and the automatic check.

Outside blockers

The ticket's Jira Blocks links, read at triage and refreshed by the pass, never by desk-q.
