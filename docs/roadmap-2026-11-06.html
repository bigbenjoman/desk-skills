Phase 2 · Evaluate other models, one at a time, on cost per accepted ticket

weeks 2–5Filed · DS-535 to DS-538

This replaces buying ChatGPT Pro 200. Changed at 14:00 on 9 Nov: all four candidates now run on one month of OpenCode Go Plus ($40), not four separate plans. Go serves every candidate to Claude Code on its Anthropic-compatible endpoint, says none of them is used for training and keeps 0 days of data, and doesn't ban scripted use. The direct plans couldn't offer that: Kimi bans scripts without written permission, Z.ai's terms don't mention training, and Google trains on Antigravity code and lets reviewers read it unless telemetry is off. Only the winner's direct plan is bought afterwards, and the bench measures whether it gives more per £ than Go. Each candidate runs on the same frozen tasks as Opus, through the same harness, and is judged on one figure: accepted tasks per pound of subscription, as long as quality holds. Leaderboard scores pick the order to test them in (see How the candidates compare), but they don't decide anything. Codex stays as a reviewer on your current plan.

What the numbers say

  • Kimi K3.1 Pro is the only candidate close to Claude on agentic coding. It has the top Terminal-Bench 4.0 score of all seven. It's well behind Opus on FrontierCode 2.0 (25.5% vs 46.4%) and Terminal-Bench-Science, and it's the slowest: 32 tokens a second and 38 minutes per Index task.
  • Gemini 4 Argon is the strongest candidate overall (Index 50.4), with by far the lowest hallucination rate (4.3%). Whether Google AI Pro gives it to the CLI is unclear. Gemini 3.8 Flash, which AI Pro certainly includes, scores 19.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0.
  • DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is the cheapest per task by a long way ($0.60 against Opus's $10.04) and beats Kimi on FrontierCode 2.0. It's also weak on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and hallucinates the most (64.1%).
  • GLM‑5.3 is behind Kimi on almost every row, and its plan costs the most of the four.

Why these don't settle it

  • The cost rows use API prices. We pay through subscriptions, and each provider sets its allowance differently. Low token use per task (Kimi uses about half of Opus's output tokens) suggests an allowance lasts longer, but only a bench run measures it.
  • The figure that decides it is accepted desk tasks per pound of subscription (first on Go Plus, then on the winner's direct plan), on our own frozen tasks, at a blind quality score no lower than Opus's. The table sets the order of the trials, and the bench makes the decision.
  • A check for each trial: Kimi's slow output and its pricing page's warning that K3.1 Pro "may queue or fail at launch" both cut tasks per 5-hour window. The bench measures queueing and failed runs too.
