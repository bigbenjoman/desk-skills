The phases

Each phase changes one thing at a time, keeps every quality gate, and ends with a measurement that decides the next phase.

Phase 0 · Levers, trialled and kept by desk itself

nowdesk-skills on auto since 21 Nov

Epic DS-631 replaces the one-lever-at-a-time plan. Jim's review on 13 Nov showed why the manual plan couldn't work: desk changes too fast between weeks (362 commits in the 7 days before), the ticket mix swings cost more than a lever does, and 7 levers would take about 6 months to get through. Now each ticket flips a coin per low-risk lever at admission and keeps the result. Once a day desk adopts, keeps testing or reverts each lever, and posts the decision as a line in the digest and in desk-q. See Self-tuning Levers for the design and Lever Analytics for the page. You chose its launch point on 14 Nov: a Levers line on desk-q's home screen, opened with v, using standard WBR charts.

  • merged, #871 · 21 Nov DS-632 to DS-646: an outcome row per ticket, a label on every park, review findings counted per round, a 14-day watch for reverts after merge, levers fixed per ticket, the auto / pinned / none row with a freeze for the whole Mac, the lever registry, the controller, instant reverts, the digest line, and DS-643 and DS-644, which had to land before any repo goes to auto.
  • merged DS-651 to DS-654 lever analytics on desk-q, and DS-692 and DS-709.
  • admitted today DS-783 the blind audit takes a worker slot. Without it, codex, review-reuse and effort are never adopted automatically. DS-696 (P4) the model check reads the CLI's model list.
  • put off Post-merge checks on DS-645 and DS-646: an audit worker on a real ticket, codex in desk-review, and a real worker log.
  • DS-443 · 4 open DS-705 (running), and DS-693, DS-694 and DS-699 (all queued).

Gate: a lever is adopted only when its own measure clearly improves, cost per admitted ticket is no more than 5% worse, and merge rate, fix tries, parks, CI reds and P0/P1 findings hold. That takes 30 to 40 tickets on each side. At about 25 tickets a week, desk-skills alone needs about 4 weeks to see a 20% saving. Jim recommended adding the work repos only after desk-skills has adopted or reverted its first lever. Baseline (6–13 Nov, no levers): 24 accepted tickets at $17.52 API-equivalent each, and 0.13 fix tries per ticket.

What the numbers say

  • Kimi K3.1 Pro is the only candidate close to Claude on agentic coding. It has the top Terminal-Bench 4.0 score of all seven. It's well behind Opus on FrontierCode 2.0 (25.5% vs 46.4%) and Terminal-Bench-Science, and it's the slowest: 32 tokens a second and 38 minutes per Index task.
  • Gemini 4 Argon is the strongest candidate overall (Index 50.4), with by far the lowest hallucination rate (4.3%). Whether Google AI Pro gives it to the CLI is unclear. Gemini 3.8 Flash, which AI Pro certainly includes, scores 19.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0.
  • DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is the cheapest per task by a long way ($0.60 against Opus's $10.04) and beats Kimi on FrontierCode 2.0. It's also weak on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and hallucinates the most (64.1%).
  • GLM‑5.3 is behind Kimi on almost every row, and its plan costs the most of the four.

Why these don't settle it

  • The cost rows use API prices. We pay through subscriptions, and each provider sets its allowance differently. Low token use per task (Kimi uses about half of Opus's output tokens) suggests an allowance lasts longer, but only a bench run measures it.
  • The figure that decides it is accepted desk tasks per pound of subscription (first on Go Plus, then on the winner's direct plan), on our own frozen tasks, at a blind quality score no lower than Opus's. The table sets the order of the trials, and the bench makes the decision.
  • A check for each trial: Kimi's slow output and its pricing page's warning that K3.1 Pro "may queue or fail at launch" both cut tasks per 5-hour window. The bench measures queueing and failed runs too.
